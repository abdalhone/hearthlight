# Hearthlight — the lore of Halcyre

*The story bible for Hearthlight: the world, its magic, its dungeons, its history, the village of Hearthvale and everyone in it, Lyra's life, the stranger from the mountain, and the mysteries the game will pull on. Where this file and older placeholder text disagree, this file wins.*

A note on the shape of it: this is a world where people live close together because the open land is not safe. Every village is a lamp in a large dark room. The story of Hearthlight is what happens inside one of those lamps, Hearthvale, to a woman named Lyra who was born there, and to a stranger the village carried down from a mountain.

---

## 1. The world

### Halcyre

The world is called **Halcyre**. The word is older than any kingdom; it comes from the speech of the Lanternwrights, the mage-builders of the deep past, and it means something like *the lit hall*. They believed the world was one great hall and that their work was to keep its lamps burning. For a long time they did. Then the lamps went out, one by one, and the hall became what it is now: a wide, beautiful, dangerous country where the light lives only in small, kept places.

The part of Halcyre where Hearthlight happens is the **Lowmarch**: the long middle country that the King's Road crosses on its way from the sea to the mountains. To the south and west lies the coast, with the walled harbor town of Saltmere and, far out across the water, the Glasswater Isles. To the north rise the **Crownfells**, a wall of grey mountains whose nearest and highest peak, the **Hollowcrown**, can be seen from Hearthvale's square on a clear morning, its broken summit white even in summer. Beyond the Crownfells is the **Pale Reach**, a land of snow and aurora that most maps do not bother to draw. Between the coast and the mountains are valleys, forests, moors, marshes and rivers, and, scattered through them like coals in a dark grate, the villages.

### Why the world is separated into villages

Because the magic in Halcyre is loose.

Long ago the Lanternwrights held the world's magic in a single great working they called the Song, a ward over everything that kept wild power as tame and still as water in a cistern. When the Song broke (the people of the Lowmarch call that time the **Unlighting**), the magic did not vanish. It spilled. It runs through the open land now the way weather does, and it is called, simply, **the Drift**.

The Drift is not evil. It is not anything. It is power without a purpose, moving over the land, and it changes what it touches. Villagers talk about it the way sailors talk about the sea, with a vocabulary for every mood of it:

- **Glimmer** is the mildest drift-weather: a sparkling haze at dusk that makes lamps burn blue and dogs howl. It does little harm, and children love it.
- **The grey** is a fog that makes things forget themselves. Fences forget to stand. People forget errands, names, and, if it lasts long enough, why they ever kept their hearths lit. A long grey is how villages die.
- **Hollow wind** is a dry, cold wind that steals warmth. It puts out candles, chills the blood, and leaves the strong shivering and the weak dead in their beds.
- **Brightfall** is the strangest: light that falls like snow on still winter nights. Where it settles, odd things grow. Flowers bloom in frost. Beasts give birth out of season. Sometimes people do too.
- **The turning** is what villagers call the Drift's effect on animals. Turned wolves grow bold and hunt in larger packs; turned boars grow vicious; still water that sits too long in drift-weather thickens, stirs and crawls away as a **slime**.

And then there are the **dungeons** (Section 3): places underground where the old magic pooled, which wake and sleep on their own slow tides and send out things that should not walk in daylight.

No one can live in the Drift for long. So people live inside **wards**.

### How villages protect themselves

A ward is a boundary that the Drift cannot easily cross. Inside it, weather is just weather, wolves are just wolves, and water stays water. Every village in the Lowmarch exists because it has one. Most of them are built the same way, out of pieces of the old world and the habits of the new:

- **The wardstone.** At the heart of every ward is a stone the Lanternwrights shaped and left behind, still humming faintly with a fragment of the old Song. Some are standing stones; some are lintels; Hearthvale's lies at the bottom of the well in the town square and is called the **Wellstone**. Nobody alive can make a wardstone. Every village that has one is built where one was found.
- **Kept flame.** A wardstone alone is a cold, weak thing. It is fed by fire that is kept with intent, never allowed to go out, and offered to it. In Hearthvale every household keeps a flame burning in the Chapel of the Hearth, and the chapel stands beside the well; their light, together, is the **Hearthlight**, and it is what holds the ward up. A village with more hearths has a stronger ward, up to a point: every stone can bear only so many flames before it cracks, and the number is a village's most important secret.
- **The keepers.** A **wardwright** is a mage who binds herself to a stone, tends it, mends it, and feels it in her own body. Not every village has one; the lucky ones do. A **chapel keeper** tends the family flames and the rites. A **lamplighter** walks the lamps at dusk, because a ward is weakest where it is dark. A **wardrunner** goes outside to check the edges, read the weather, and bring warning.
- **Customs.** Ward-chalk on every threshold, renewed at the new moon. Cold iron nails over doors. A bell whose sound marks the ward's reach: in Hearthvale, the ward runs about as far as the chapel bell can be heard on a still day, which villagers call **the bell-reach**. The thin outer band where the ward fades is called **the hem**. Nobody leaves the ward alone. Nobody lets a hearth go out. Strangers spend a night under the chapel roof before they are let in. And nobody spills blood inside a ward if they can help it, because violence inside the bell-reach thins it, and everyone knows it.

### Travel and roads

Most people in the Lowmarch never travel farther than the hem of their own ward. Those who do travel carry a **road-candle**, a flame taken from their home hearth in a shuttered lantern, which keeps a little circle of ward around them as long as it burns. They travel in groups, by day, in the **still weeks** when the weather-wise say the Drift is quiet, and they never camp where they cannot see a waystone.

The **King's Road** runs from Saltmere on the coast to the foot of the Crownfells. It was laid four hundred years ago by a king who dreamed of joining the villages into one country again, and along its length he raised or restored **waystones**: tall pillars with a lamp niche in the top. A waystone with a lit lamp throws a small ward across the road, enough for a caravan to shelter under for a night. Perhaps one in three still works. The rest are dead stone, and the stretches between live ones are where carts disappear. North of Hearthvale the road fades into a track, and then into a rumor.

Merchants like Garrick Hale, wardrunners like Hess Morrow, pilgrims, couriers and the desperate are the only regular travelers. A letter takes weeks. News arrives with the carts and is old by the time it is told.

### Other villages

Villagers speak of other places the way you speak of distant cousins: with affection, suspicion and a fund of half-true stories.

- **Brightwater** — a river town three days south-east by road, or two days down the river Bright by the ferry that used to run from Mirror Lake. Big enough to have two chapels and a market, and a ward strong enough to have walls inside it. Tobias's wife June is there "visiting her sister". People who go to Brightwater for a season have a way of staying.
- **Saltmere** — the harbor town on the coast, twelve days away, the closest thing the Lowmarch has to a city. A guild of delvers, a mint that once struck coins for kings, a library, and a sea-wall carved with the names of drowned sailors. Garrick's mother lives there. Lyra's oldest friend Bryn went there and did not come back.
- **Cinderwick** — a mining village in the eastern hills whose wardstone sits in a coal seam that has been burning, slowly and on purpose, for two hundred years. Cinderwick folk are black-handed, proud and blunt, and they make the best lamp-oil in the Lowmarch.
- **Owlsend** — the last village on the King's Road before the mountains, small, stubborn and poor. Its lamps could be seen from the shoulder of the Hollowcrown on clear nights. Hess Morrow says that this summer they could not.
- **Brindlecombe** — a dead village a day and a half west, its houses still standing with their doors open. Its ward failed nineteen years ago in a winter-long grey that the Lowmarch remembers as **the Greywinter**. What happened at Hearthvale's ward line that winter is something the older villagers do not talk about (see Sections 4 and 9).
- **The Glasswater Isles** — islands far to the south-west across the sea, where the sand is like crushed glass and the lagoon glows on summer nights. No one in Hearthvale has been there except Maren Ashgrove, who was born there.

## 2. Magic

There are all kinds of magic in Halcyre, because magic is everywhere: loose in the Drift, pooled in the dungeons, humming in the old stones, sleeping in certain plants and certain people. What differs is how people reach it, what it asks of them, and what their neighbors think of them for doing it. Villagers do not have one word for "magic". They have a word for each kind, and opinions about all of them.

### Hearthcraft — the magic of kept flame and boundaries

**How it works.** Hearthcraft is the craft of wards: of saying *here, and not past here*. It works through fire that is kept on purpose, lines that are drawn on purpose, and stones that remember the Song. A household chalk-mark over a door is hearthcraft. So is the chapel's ring of family flames, and so is a wardwright's binding to the Wellstone.

**Cost and risk.** Hearthcraft binds the one who practices it to a place. A family that lets its chapel flame go out feels it: its house goes cold in a way no fire fixes until the flame is relit. A wardwright pays most. Bound to a stone, she feels every crack in the ward as an ache in her own bones and cannot go more than a day's walk from the stone without falling sick. It is a marriage, and villagers call it that: *marrying the stone*.

**Who and how common.** Everyone does a little of it. A real wardwright is rare; perhaps one village in four has one. Hearthvale's was Maren Ashgrove. Since she vanished, Sister Aveline has kept the stone fed with the chapel rites, but Aveline is not a mage, and the ward is thinning.

**How villagers feel.** Hearthcraft is the only magic everyone trusts. It is also the one they most expect someone else to carry for them.

### Lumencraft — the Lamp-art

**How it works.** Lumencraft is the art of shaping light into force. A lumencrafter gathers the warmth in her own body, kindles it into light, and gives the light a shape: a bolt, a shield, a lamp that hangs in the air, a ring that holds. It is the most "mage-like" of the magics, the one in the stories, and it is studied: it takes years of teaching, star charts, breath-work and a great deal of failing.

**Cost and risk.** Light is made out of the caster's own warmth. Every spell takes a little heat from her body; a long fight leaves a lumencrafter blue-lipped and shaking, a condition called **lamp-chill**. Push past it and she **gutters**: she faints, cold as stone, and if no one warms her she may not wake. This is why lumencrafters love fires, wool, strong tea and moonpetals, and why the good ones carry a **heartglass**: a glow crystal set into a staff, which holds warmth the way a bank holds coin, so she can spend from it instead of from her own blood.

**Who and how common.** Rare. A village is lucky to see one in a generation. The towns have a few, the Saltmere delvers' guild has more, and most of them came to it the way Lyra did: the light woke in them by itself, and someone who knew the Art happened to be close enough to teach them before they hurt themselves.

**How villagers feel.** Admiration with a little fear in it. A lumencrafter can light a lamp on a hollow-wind night, mend a wound, and burn a wolf out of the dark. She can also, if she is angry, do the same to a person.

**Where Lyra's magic sits.** Lyra is a lumencrafter, trained by Maren from the age of eleven. Her three workings are:

- **Sparks** (the game's arcane bolts) — the simplest lumencraft there is, light thrown hard enough to strike. Lyra's burn violet, which is unusual; Maren's were white. Each one costs a breath of warmth.
- **Lending warmth** (her healing spell) — the hardest. She pours warmth and light into someone else's body to speed its own mending. It costs her more than anything else she does, because she is giving away exactly what she is made of. Maren told her: *never lend more than you can live without.* Lyra learned it harder and earlier than Maren wanted, for reasons in Section 7.
- **The Stillring** (Binding Light) — Maren's own invention, and the thing that makes Maren's school unusual. It is lumencraft shaped by hearthcraft: a ward drawn around one body instead of one village. A ring of violet light closes around someone's feet and, for a few heartbeats, the world says *not past here* to them. It does not hurt. It does not last. Maren called it *the polite way to say stop*. Lyra uses it exactly that way: to make someone stop, so that she can choose what happens next.

Her staff is ash wood shod in iron (the ferrule was Tilly Ashby's first paid work at the smithy), and it is tipped with a heartglass Maren gave her on her sixteenth birthday, which Maren had taken from the upper halls of the Sunken Ruins, with the Moot's leave, in her first year in Hearthvale. When the staff glows at night, that is the crystal giving back warmth.

### Greenwit — hedge-magic and the quickened herb

**How it works.** Some plants hold magic: moonpetals, which open only at night and drink moonlight; silverleaf, which knits flesh; blue ruin-lichen, which glows when breathed on; lindenflower honey, which keeps for a hundred years. Greenwit is the craft of knowing them, gathering them at the right hour, and brewing them so that their magic wakes in a body. A greenwitch does not cast; she prepares. Mira Fennick would never call herself a mage. She is the best one in the valley.

**Cost and risk.** Slow and safe, mostly. The risk is in the gathering (moonpetals bloom in the hem of the ward, where wolves walk at night) and in mistakes: drift-touched herbs look the same as clean ones and can sour a potion into poison.

**Who and how common.** Every village has someone. Every grandmother knows a little.

**How villagers feel.** Gratitude, and a faint condescension they would deny. Greenwit is "just herbs", until you need it.

Moonpetals matter to everything in this book. They warm the cold-handed, ease lamp-chill, and soothe a guttered mage back to waking. Maren bought Mira's strongest moonpetal salve before she went into the ruins, "for cold hands in deep places". Elda puts crushed moonpetal in her honey cakes, which is why Lyra, who spends warmth for a living, cannot get enough of them, though she has not yet worked out why.

### Forgesong — iron that remembers

**How it works.** Some smiths, mostly without meaning to, put something into iron as they work it: a ring, a note, a stubbornness. A forgesung blade bites slimes and turned beasts as if they were cloth; a forgesung nail over a door holds a ward-chalk line through a hollow wind. The old smiths say you do it by listening to the iron as it cools and refusing to strike until it is ready. Bram Ironhand does exactly this, and would be offended to hear it called magic.

**Cost and risk.** Only time and temper. A smith who works angry makes brittle steel.

**Who and how common.** A handful of families, passed down like a cough. The Ironhands are one.

**How villagers feel.** They don't think about it. They just know that Bram's blades are better.

The stone of the Sunken Ruins rings like an anvil when struck, and Bram is right to think that means something. The Lanternwrights forged as well as sang.

### The Old Song — Lanternwright songcraft

**How it works.** No one knows anymore. The Lanternwrights sang their workings into stone, and the stones still hum: wardstones, waystones, and the great rings called **waygates** that once crossed the world in a single step. Their script survives carved on ruins, and a few scholars, Maren among them, can read a little of it. A few fragments of the Song can be hummed or chanted and still do something.

**Cost and risk.** Unknown, which is the risk. Maren believed the Song does not cost the singer warmth, as lumencraft does, but *place*: that whoever sings deep enough becomes part of what is sung.

**Who and how common.** Almost no one. Maren. Perhaps, now, the stranger from the mountain (see Section 8), though nobody knows that yet.

**How villagers feel.** Uneasy reverence. The Song made the world safe once, and then failed.

### Driftcraft — the brightborn

**How it works.** It isn't a craft. Some people are touched by the Drift, born under brightfall or caught in glimmer as children, and come away with something: a knack for finding water, eyes that see in the dark, hair the color of a night sky, or a magic that wakes too strong and too early. They are called **brightborn** or, less kindly, **drift-touched**.

**Cost and risk.** No one knows what the Drift gives with one hand and takes with the other. Brightborn gifts are wild and hard to steer. Some burn out young. Some are simply a little odd, all their lives.

**Who and how common.** Uncommon, and mostly hidden, because:

**How villagers feel.** Suspicion. The old women make warding signs. In bad years, villages have put brightborn children outside the ward. Hearthvale has not done that in living memory, and Petra Ashby would like to see them try.

People in Hearthvale have whispered for twenty-four years that Lyra Ambrel, born to a fifty-two-year-old mother in the Brightfall winter, with hair that grew in blue, is brightborn. Lyra knows they whisper it. She has decided not to care. Whether it is true is one of the mysteries in Section 9.

### Gravelight — the dungeons' own magic

**How it works.** Deep in waking dungeons the pooled magic acquires something like hunger, and it wears whatever it finds. It wears the bones of the dead and walks them as **skeletons**. It wears darkness and flies it as **bats**. Its light is cold and blue, and it is called **gravelight**. A few people have tried to learn to use it. The stories about them all end badly.

**Cost and risk.** Everything. Gravelight is not given; it is borrowed from something that wants it back.

**How villagers feel.** Horror. It is the one magic forbidden by every Moot in the Lowmarch.

### Small charms — everybody's magic

Then there is the magic everyone does without thinking of it as magic: knots tied in a sweetheart's hair-ribbon, salt across a sill, a bell on a goat, humming to the fish (Corran Dell swears by it), a spoon of honey left out on Longwatch night, a word said over bread. Most of it does nothing at all. Some of it does a little. All of it makes people feel braver in the dark, which in Halcyre is half of what any magic is for.

## 3. Dungeons

### What they are

When the Song broke, the world's magic spilled, and like spilled water it ran downhill. Most of it became the Drift, moving over the land. But some of it ran down, into the low places: caves, mine-workings, cellars, the vaults of drowned temples and, above all, into the buried works of the Lanternwrights, which were made to hold magic and still do. There it pooled, and the pools became **dungeons**: places where magic gathers so thickly that it starts to behave like something alive.

The Lowmarch has dozens. Most are small and asleep. A few are large and famous: the **Deepvein** under the Cinderwick hills, over whose crystal-seams the Border Wars were fought; the **Drowned Choir** that the Saltmere delvers work at low tide. Hearthvale has two near it: the **Sunken Ruins**, and a small one called the **Weeping Cut** under Stonecut Quarry, which the Moot sealed with iron stakes five years ago (Section 7 tells why).

### How they wake and sleep

Dungeons breathe, very slowly. For years, sometimes generations, a dungeon sleeps: its halls are cold, its stone is quiet, and the brave and the greedy can walk in and carry out crystals and old coins. Then something wakes it. A strong drift-weather. A disturbance at its heart. Someone opening a door that was shut on purpose. Waking takes weeks, and has signs: lights moving inside at night, stone that hums when you stand still, slimes in nearby pools, animals that will not graze near it. A waking dungeon pushes its creatures out into the world. A fully woken dungeon is a place no sensible person enters.

### What lives in them

Dungeons do not breed creatures. They make them out of what they have.

- **Slimes** are water that sat too long in magic. They gather in dungeon pools, in quarry rain-holes, along lake shores where the ward is thin, and in meadow puddles after drift-weather. They are slow, stupid, acidic, and their gel is a fine burn salve.
- **Bats** are the dungeon's darkness given wings. They pour out of waking dungeons at night.
- **Skeletons** are the dead, worn. A dungeon's hunger finds bones (of pilgrims, delvers, the old keepers) and walks them. They are not the people they were. They do not remember. They guard, and they walk the halls at night, and their joints rattle against stone.
- **Glow crystals** are not creatures but the dungeon's own light, cooled and set hard. They grow where magic pools longest. They are the most valuable thing a dungeon gives: a heartglass for a mage's staff, a reserve of warmth for a ward in a hard winter, a treasure for a smith. Bram pays better for crystals than for ore, and he is right to.

### What delving means

To **delve** is to go into a dungeon on purpose and come out with something. In the towns it is a trade with a guild, rules, and a widows' fund. In the villages it is forbidden without the Moot's leave, because a delver who wakes a dungeon wakes it for everyone. Delvers go in pairs or more, with road-candles, rope, a mage if they can afford one, and an agreement about when to turn back. The old delvers' rule is: *the deep pays the one who leaves first.*

### What the Sunken Ruins truly are

To the village, the Sunken Ruins are an old walled temple half swallowed by the marsh southwest of Hearthvale: a place of fallen pillars, blue lichen and stone that rings when struck, locked behind an iron gate since before anyone's grandmother. Sister Aveline says it was "a temple to a fire no one remembers", sealed by its own people. She is right, as far as that goes.

The truth goes deeper. The Sunken Ruins were a **verse-house** of the Lanternwrights: one of the places where they kept a part of the Song burning. Its "forgotten fire" was the **Undying Lamp**, a flame sung into stone, and below it, at the bottom of the temple's stair, is a **waygate**, a ring of stone that is not dead. When the Song broke, the verse-house sank into the marsh and its keepers, the last Lanternwrights who served there, did not flee. They sealed the temple from the inside and went down, and what happened to them is part of the hidden truth in Section 9.

For centuries the ruins slept. Pilgrims left coins at a shrine in the upper halls, until a hundred and ten years ago three of them went lower and did not come back, and the Moot locked the gate and set a warden's toll-house beside it. The warden is long gone. The key hung in the empty toll-house until this spring, when the bandits took it.

The ruins began to wake at midsummer, the night Maren Ashgrove went in. Blue gravelight moves in them after dark. Skeletons walk the halls. Bats pour out at dusk. The stone hums, low and slow, like something breathing. Deepest inside, in the room at the foot of the stair, Maren left her journal.

## 4. History

Hearthvale counts years from its own founding; the rest of the Lowmarch counts them however it likes. Dates below are given as "years ago", from the autumn the game begins.

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| **The Age of Lanterns** (beyond counting) | The Lanternwrights sing the Song and hold the world's magic still. They raise wardstones, waystones and waygates, build verse-houses where parts of the Song are kept burning, and call the world Halcyre, the lit hall. In the far north, beyond the Crownfells, they sing something to sleep that they could not kill. |
| **The Unlighting** (about a thousand years ago) | The Song breaks. Nobody knows why. Magic spills over the land as the Drift and runs down into the dungeons. Verse-houses sink; the Sunken Ruins go down into the marsh with their keepers sealed inside. The Lanternwrights vanish from the world. |
| **The Hearth Years** (the centuries after) | Survivors gather around fires near the old stones and learn, by trial and grief, that kept flame wakes a wardstone. The first villages. The Hearth faith: *a candle does not ask the dark for permission.* |
| **The Crowned Road** (about 400 years ago) | King Aldwin the Lampbearer, of a line out of Saltmere, lays the King's Road from the coast to the Crownfells and relights its waystones. For a hundred and fifty years the Lowmarch is something like a kingdom. |
| **Hearthvale founded** (312 years ago) | A caravan caught in a hollow wind shelters in the lee of a hill beside the road. One kettle-fire, tended all night by a widow named Gerda Mead, does not go out. In the morning they find a well-shaft beside it and a living wardstone at its bottom. Gerda's fire becomes the hearth of the Ember Kettle. A wandering mage called Wend the Grey wakes the stone; a chandler named Ansel Ambrel makes the first family candles and lights the first lamps. His descendants have lit Hearthvale's lamps ever since. |
| **The Guttering** (about 250 years ago) | Half the King's Road waystones fail in a single winter. The crown's couriers stop coming. The last king dies without an heir, and the Lowmarch goes back to being villages. The road keeps the name. Saltmere's mint goes on striking the old crown pennies, stamped with a lamp, for another century and a half. |
| **The ruins locked** (110 years ago) | Three pilgrims go below the upper shrine of the Sunken Ruins and are never seen again. The Moot locks the iron gate and builds a warden's toll-house beside it. The Saltmere mint closes ten years later; the lamp-pennies pilgrims left in the ruins are the last of their kind in the valley. |
| **The road-candle** (60 years ago) | Aveline's predecessor, Mother Rhoswen, lights a candle in the chapel for "whoever is still on the road". It has not gone out since. |
| **The Border Wars** (35 to 31 years ago) | Saltmere and the hill-holds around Cinderwick fight over the crystal-seams of the Deepvein dungeon. Young men walk off from every village along the road. Rowan Vale's father is one. Aveline serves as a battle-cleric. |
| **Aveline comes** (30 years ago) | A tired woman who will not talk about the war takes the Chapel of the Hearth. Bram Ironhand, twenty-two, takes his father's forge the same year. |
| **The Brightfall winter** (25 years ago) | For eleven still nights light falls on the valley like snow. Moonpetals bloom in frost. Wenna Ambrel, fifty-one and long past hope of children, is with child by spring. |
| **Lyra born** (24 years ago) | Lyra Ambrel is born at midsummer to Hobb, fifty-five, and Wenna, fifty-two. Petra Ashby delivers her. |
| **Elda takes the Kettle** (22 years ago) | Elda Thornbury comes inland from Saltmere, where she had stopped watching the harbor for her husband's ship, to take over her aunt's inn. |
| **The Greywinter** (19 years ago) | A grey settles on the west of the valley for a whole winter. Brindlecombe's people forget their flames, and their ward fails. Some sixty survivors walk a day and a half through the grey to Hearthvale's ward line. The Moot lets in fourteen, mostly children, and sends the rest on toward Brightwater. Many die on that road. Among those let in is Sabeth Coll, fifteen. Among those turned away is her brother Varek, seventeen. |
| **Maren comes** (19 years ago) | The Wellstone, worn thin by the Greywinter, cracks along one face. The Moot sends to Saltmere for a wardwright. Maren Ashgrove, of the Glasswater Isles, answers, takes the room at the top of the Ember Kettle, marries the stone, and stays. |
| **Lyra's magic wakes** (13 years ago) | On Longwatch night an eleven-year-old Lyra lights every lamp on the square at once in violet fire. Maren takes her as apprentice. |
| **Rowan made captain** (11 years ago) | Rowan Vale, twenty-seven, is made captain of the watch. |
| **Hanna Ironhand dies** (6 years ago) | Winter fever. Bram sets out two cups at breakfast from then on. |
| **The Weeping Cut** (5 years ago) | Three young people go into the small dungeon under Stonecut Quarry without the Moot's leave. All three come out; not all of them come out whole. The Moot seals the Cut with forgesung iron. |
| **The Brightwater ferry** (2 winters ago) | A hollow wind on the river Bright overturns Corran Dell's ferry. Pip's parents drown; Corran pulls Pip out. Elda takes Pip in that same night. Corran has not run the ferry since. |
| **The bandits come** (last autumn) | A band led by a one-eyed man with an axe settles in the hills northeast of the village and starts taking a "toll" from carts on the King's Road. |
| **The ambush** (this spring) | Two of Rowan's watch, Davin Morrow and Edric Hale, are killed in a bandit ambush on the King's Road. Weeks later the bandits raid the old toll-house by the Sunken Ruins and carry off the rusted gate key. |
| **Maren goes in** (three months ago, at midsummer) | At dusk, with a lantern and a coil of rope, Maren Ashgrove walks toward the Sunken Ruins and goes over the wall. She does not come back. That night blue lights begin to move in the ruins. |
| **The stranger found** (two days later) | Lyra and Hess Morrow find a stranger lying in the snow inside the stone ring on the shoulder of the Hollowcrown, and carry them down to Hearthvale. |
| **The long sleep** (that summer) | The stranger sleeps in the chapel for eighty-one days and cannot be woken. |
| **Now** (early autumn) | The stranger has been awake for six days and has moved into the Wayhouse cottage off the square. Lyra has taken the hearthwatch on them. Two days ago, on their first walk outside the ward, wolves came at dusk on the King's Road and the stranger stood beside her. The harvest is nearly in. The Sheaf Moot is coming. The ward is thinner than anyone will say aloud. |

## 5. Hearthvale

### Its founding and its ward

Hearthvale grew up around a kettle-fire that would not go out, a well with a wardstone at the bottom, and a chandler who kept the dark back with candles. It has kept those three things at its heart for three hundred and twelve years. The Ember Kettle's hearth is Gerda Mead's fire, never once allowed to die; on Kindling Day every family in the valley relights its hearth from it. The well in the town square still gives sweet water, and on very still nights, if you lean over it, you can hear the Wellstone hum. The Chapel of the Hearth stands beside the well, and on its altar burn the family flames, one for each household, which feed the stone. The lamps around the square and along the lanes are lit every dusk by an Ambrel, as they have been since Ansel Ambrel's day.

The ward's bell-reach covers the village, Reed Farm, the near edge of Whisperwood, the shore of Mirror Lake and most of South Meadow. Stonecut Quarry sits in the hem; that is why the slimes have crept into its rain-pools. The Bandit Camp, the deep forest and the Sunken Ruins are outside it.

The Wellstone can bear forty-one family flames. Hearthvale keeps thirty-eight. That number is known to the Reeve, the chapel keeper, the lamplighter and the wardwright, and to nobody else.

### Customs and festivals

- **Dusk bell and lamplighting.** At sunset the chapel bell rings once and the lamplighter begins his round. Children who are out when the bell rings run home; it is the oldest rule in the village.
- **Threshold night.** Anyone the village lets in from outside spends their first night under the chapel roof, beside the family flames, before they may sleep anywhere else inside the ward. The stranger from the mountain spent eighty-one of them.
- **Hearthwatch.** Someone brought in from outside the ward is not left alone under a roof for their first season. A villager takes the hearthwatch, sleeps in the same house and keeps an eye on them, in case the Drift is in them. The Moot pays the watcher a stipend. The watcher may give it up at any time, by handing the house key back to the Reeve; the Moot then finds another watcher, or the found one sleeps in the guardhouse. Nobody is ever obliged to stay.
- **Kindling Day** (first thaw). Every hearth in the valley is put out at dawn and relit from the Ember Kettle's fire. The whole village carries coals home in clay pots. Children are allowed to carry the Kettle's coal for the first time at seven.
- **Moonpetal Night** (late summer). The children of the village go into the edge of Whisperwood with the watch and gather moonpetals by their glow; the best bloom goes on the altar. Pip has been talking about next year's since this year's.
- **The Sheaf Moot** (harvest, coming soon). The last sheaf from Reed Farm is burned on the well-cap as an offering to the Wellstone, the Hearthmoot meets in the Ember Kettle to settle the year's business, and there is a dance in the square. Mira hides from the dance. The Moot this year will decide two things that worry people: what to do about the bandits, and whether to ask Lyra Ambrel, formally and before everyone, to marry the stone.
- **Longwatch** (midwinter). The longest night. No one sleeps; the whole village keeps the chapel flames together, tells stories, sings the old songs, and leaves a spoon of honey on the doorstep for anyone still on the road. The lamplighter's star, which villagers call **Lyra**, rises over the Hollowcrown just before dawn.

### Its economy

Hearthvale feeds itself and sells a little. Reed Farm grows turnips and wheat. Stonecut Quarry gives building stone and iron ore for the Ironhand forge. Corran Dell fishes Mirror Lake. Wenna Ambrel's hives at the edge of Whisperwood give lindenflower honey and wax; Hobb Ambrel makes the wax into the village's candles. Mira Fennick brews potions and salves. Sabeth Coll dyes and sews. Hess Morrow brings in pelts and venison. Elda Thornbury feeds everyone, and the Ember Kettle is where coin changes hands. Garrick Hale's cart is the village's only regular road to the outside: he takes out honey, wax, blades, potions, cloth and pelts, and brings back salt, lamp-oil from Cinderwick, needles, dyes, news and lies. Coin is copper and silver, and some of it is very old.

### Its rules

Hearthvale is governed by the **Hearthmoot**: the head of every household that keeps a flame in the chapel, meeting in the Ember Kettle's common room when there is business. It elects a **Reeve** to keep its ledgers and speak for it; the Reeve is Alder Ashby. Captain Rowan Vale keeps the watch and the peace. The laws are few and old:

1. Keep your flame. A household whose chapel flame goes out through neglect loses its voice in the Moot for a year.
2. No one goes outside the ward alone, and no one goes past the wall of the Sunken Ruins at all.
3. No blood inside the bell-reach. Striking a neighbor is the worst common crime in the village, because it thins the ward for everyone; knocking someone senseless is worse; the punishment is the Moot's to set, and it has, more than once, been *outside*.
4. No delving without the Moot's leave.
5. Strangers keep threshold night and are under hearthwatch for a season.
6. What the Moot decides about the ward is not repeated outside the Moot.

### Its worries right now

The bandits are the worry people say aloud. They have stolen Bram's blades, Tobias's sheaves and two lives, and they hold the key to the ruins gate. The ruins are the worry people say quietly: lights, rattling, bats, and Maren gone into them. The ward is the worry almost no one says at all: without a wardwright it is thinning, the slimes are in the quarry, the wolves are bolder every night, and winter is coming. Then there is the stranger, who sleeps in the Wayhouse and has no past. There is the question the Moot means to put to Lyra. There is June Reed's long visit to Brightwater. And there is the harvest, which must come in whatever else happens.

### What a day sounds like

Before dawn, Rowan's boots on the bounds path, and the creak of the guardhouse door. Then Duchess, Tobias's brown hen, who believes the sun waits for her. The chapel bell at sunrise, once. Bram's anvil starting up, steady enough to set the hours by, and Tilly's lighter hammer answering it, a little off the beat. Elda's kettle screaming in the Kettle's kitchen, and Elda's voice over it. Bees along the wood's edge. Pip shouting for Biscuit. Corran's tuneless humming drifting up from the dock. Garrick crying his wares to nobody in particular. Mid-afternoon, the Reed wheat hissing in the wind, and Tobias apologizing to it. At sunset the dusk bell, the scrape of Hobb Ambrel's ladder against the first lamp-post, and doors closing all along the lane. Then the Kettle's common room: Hobb and Corran arguing over a game of stones, someone singing badly, Elda laughing. Late, a wooden flute from the guardhouse roof that everyone hears and nobody mentions. Last of all, the Wellstone humming at the bottom of the well, which you only hear if everything else is quiet.

## 6. The people

Eighteen people live in these pages: the eight villagers the game already knows, deepened, and ten more. They talk to each other when no one is listening. What they talk about is here: who they love, who they owe, what they are hiding, and what they make of Lyra and of the stranger from the mountain. Ages are as of this autumn.

### Hobb Ambrel — chandler and lamplighter, 79
*Look:* tall and stooped like a lamp-post in a wind, white tufts of hair, beeswax on his cuffs, the long brass-hooked lamplighter's pole always in hand.

**Who he is.** Gentle, slow, dry-witted, and deaf in one ear when it suits him. Hobb has lit Hearthvale's lamps every dusk for forty-one years and makes every candle that burns in the chapel. He is a man who did his duty every evening of his life and has begun, very late, to wonder what else he might have done.

**His story.** An Ambrel, born to the ladder, he broke with family custom at twenty-three and walked the King's Road for fifteen years as a road-warden, carrying road-candles for caravans beside his partner Garran Ironhand, Bram's father. He came home at thirty-eight when his own father died, took up the ladder and married Wenna, who had waited for him. They lost three babies, Ansel, Bee and little Tom, none of whom saw a second winter. Then, when he was fifty-five, Lyra.

**Wound.** The Greywinter. He counted how many flames the Wellstone could bear, and the Moot let fourteen people in and turned the rest away on his number. He has believed for nineteen years that the grey made him miscount.

**Want.** To see Lyra live by her own light. To see the sea, once.

**Secret.** He has never told Wenna about the count. And his eyes are failing at dusk; Lyra has been lighting the far lamps on Lamplight Lane before he gets to them, and he pretends not to notice.

**How he talks.** Slowly, mildly, in lamp-sayings: "Every wick wants trimming." "Well now." Calls Lyra "Star".

**His people.** *Wenna*, his wife of forty-one years: she scolds, he hums, they hold hands on the bench outside the chandlery every evening. *Corran Dell*, his rival and friend: they play stones at the Kettle every night, cheat, and accuse each other of it; Hobb has never quite forgiven Corran for courting Wenna while he was on the road, and has never once been sorry Corran is alive. *Bram*, son of his road-partner, who mends his ladder and calls him "old man". *Sabeth*, the girl he took in during the Greywinter, the daughter he got to choose. *Alder Ashby*, whom he trusts completely, which is the saddest thing in this book.

**On Lyra.** "She's the light I didn't plan. Best thing I never planned."

**On the stranger.** He hung a new lamp beside the Wayhouse door the day they woke. "Everyone found deserves a lamp."

### Wenna Ambrel — beekeeper and songkeeper, 76
*Look:* small and fierce, milky-blue clouded eyes, a straw hat with its veil pushed back, bee-stings on her hands she no longer feels.

**Who she is.** Sharp-tongued, funny, impatient and unsentimental on top; underneath, the deepest well in the village. She knows every old song in the valley by heart and sings them to her bees. She is nearly blind now and finds her hives along the Whisperwood edge by the sound of them.

**Her story.** Hearthvale-born, a beekeeper's daughter. Corran Dell courted her at twenty; she turned him down for a boy who had gone off down the King's Road and might never come back, and waited fifteen years for him. She married Hobb at thirty-five. Then three small graves. Then, at fifty-two, after the Brightfall winter, a daughter.

**Wound.** The waiting. She waited for everything, for a husband, for children, for her life to start, and believes the waiting cost her half of it.

**Want.** To know her daughter will be all right when she and Hobb are gone, and that Lyra will never wait for anything the way she did.

**Secret.** On the ninth night of the Brightfall she walked out alone past the hem into South Meadow, stood in the falling light with her three children's names in her mouth, and asked. She has never told Hobb. She does not know what answered. She knows something did.

**How she talks.** Quick, blunt, scolding love. Uses full names when she is cross: "Lyra Ambrel, don't you dare." Sings when she is moved and claims it is for the bees.

**Her people.** *Hobb.* *Petra Ashby*, her best friend and worst enemy for seventy years: they quarrel daily and would die for each other. *Corran*, who brings her the first fish of every spring and thinks she doesn't know why. *Elda*, who buys her lindenflower honey for the honey cakes and pays too much on purpose; Wenna knows, and overcharges anyway out of pride. *Sabeth*, who sews her bee-veils and whom she taught to keep bees in the Greywinter year, so the girl would have something alive to look after.

**On Lyra.** "I told her the day she moved out: don't you dare stay anywhere for our sake. Not in this house, not in this village, not beside anybody. You hear me?"

**On the stranger.** Wary. "The mountain gives and the mountain takes, and it never gives for nothing." She has asked to touch their face, to see them the only way she can.

### Elda Thornbury — innkeeper of the Ember Kettle, 58
*Look:* stout and rosy, silver hair in a bun, a cream apron over rose skirts, earrings, a ladle she points like a sceptre.

**Who she is.** Warm, motherly, a gossip, generous to a fault, a worrier. The Ember Kettle is her kingdom, its fire is three hundred years old, and everyone in it is her family whether they like it or not.

**Her story.** Born in Saltmere. Married a sailor, Tam, who wrote every month for nine years; then the letters stopped. She came inland twenty-two years ago to take over her aunt's inn, partly so she would stop watching the harbor. She kept the room at the top of the house for Maren Ashgrove for nineteen years. Lyra lodged under her eaves from twenty to twenty-four. She took Pip in the night the ferry went down.

**Wound.** Tam. **Want.** A full table, and nobody else leaving. **Secret.** The lantern she lights in the top window every night for Tam. The moonpetal in the honey cakes. And a new one: the half-written letter on Maren's desk begins *Little lamp*. Elda saw that much, laid a cloth over it, and has not told Lyra, because she is frightened of what the rest of it says.

**How she talks.** "Love", "dear", "sit, sit", and gossip that begins "don't tell her I told you, but…"

**Her people.** *Pip*, her sparrow. *Garrick*, whose stories she never believes and always asks for another of. *Wenna*, her honey. *Mira*, whom she is determined to marry off to the Captain. *Tobias*, about whom she whispers that June's "visit" is going the way of Maudie Ashby's. *Rowan*, whose watch she feeds for free.

**On Lyra.** "Four years under my roof and she never once let me pay for a thing she could pay for herself. That's her all over. Eats like a bird."

**On the stranger.** "Our foundling." She spooned broth into them every day of the long sleep and has decided, firmly, to love them.

### Bram Ironhand — blacksmith, 52
*Look:* big, brown-skinned, grey-bearded, soot to the elbows, a leather apron and a hammer that never seems to leave his hand.

**Who he is.** Gruff, slow to speak and quick to help, stubborn and loyal. His blades are forgesung, though he would grunt at the word.

**His story.** His father Garran lit the Ironhand forge after years on the road beside Hobb Ambrel; Bram took it at twenty-two, thirty years ago, and it has not gone cold since. His wife Hanna died of the winter fever six years ago. Five years ago the Moot asked him to forge the iron stakes that sealed the Weeping Cut; he made them and did not ask why, because he already knew whose daughter had nearly died in there.

**Wound.** Hanna. He still sets out two cups at breakfast. **Want.** To say one true thing aloud before he dies, instead of only writing it. **Secret.** The poems in the tin box under the anvil. He does not know Tilly has read them.

**How he talks.** Short, blunt sentences. "Hmph." Forge talk. Softens, almost shyly, around Pip.

**His people.** *Pip*, for whom he leaves tin animals on the Kettle windowsill. *Tilly*, his apprentice, too loud, too quick, the best pair of hands he has ever trained; he has not told her so. *Hobb*, "the old man", whose ladder he mends every spring. *Aveline*, who sat with him the night Hanna died. *Rowan*, who never oils the sword Bram made him. *Mira*, whose burn salve he pretends not to need.

**On Lyra.** After Davin Morrow's funeral she came into the smithy and cried, and he gave her tea and said nothing for an hour, and they have trusted each other since. "Asks good questions. Doesn't waste words. Hmph. Good."

**On the stranger.** He has held the sword they were found with. It is a metal he cannot name, and it rings like the stone of the Sunken Ruins. "Old. Older than my father's father. Someone made that who knew what they were doing. I want to know who."

### Pip — orphan, 9
*Look:* a small ginger tornado: wild curls, freckles, a green shirt with one elbow out, enormous blue eyes.

**Who he is.** Curious, fearless, chatty and a bit of a menace. Under the bravery is a boy who lost his parents and is terrified of losing anyone else.

**His story.** Pip Tarrow, son of Ned Tarrow the cooper and his wife Lissy, both drowned when the Brightwater ferry overturned two winters ago. Corran Dell pulled Pip out of the river. Elda took him in that same night. He follows a ginger stray he calls Biscuit, practices "spells" with a stick in the square, and has a hideout behind the chapel woodpile with glass beads and a wolf tooth.

**Wound.** Deep water. He will not go onto the dock. **Want.** To be a mage. **Secret.** He and Tansy Reed sneak out to the hem of South Meadow at night to watch the blue lights in the ruins. And he is afraid Lyra will go into the ruins like Maren did and not come back.

**How he talks.** Breathless, too many questions at once. "Whoa!" "Is that TRUE?" "Miss Lyra" when polite, "Lyra! Lyra!" when not.

**His people.** *Elda*, "like a mum". *Bram*, the secret tin-animal maker (Pip knows). *Rowan*, the bravest person ever, except Lyra. *Tansy*, his partner in crime, who pretends he is a nuisance. *Corran*, whom he likes and cannot quite look at. *Tobias*, who pays him a copper a boar track, some of which are real.

**On Lyra.** "She's the best mage in the WHOLE world and she's going to teach me." (She has said: when you're eleven. The age she was.)

**On the stranger.** Certain they are a knight from the old stories, or a fallen star, or both. Follows them. Asks them everything.

### Captain Rowan Vale — captain of the watch, 38
*Look:* auburn hair and moustache, a scar across the jaw, iron mail under a blue tabard, a sword he never oils.

**Who he is.** Stern, dutiful, tireless; he carries the safety of Hearthvale like a pack nobody else may touch. Kinder than he lets on, and blind to the one person who worries about him most.

**His story.** His father, Ewan Vale, marched off to the Border Wars when Rowan was five and never came home; his mother waited at the door for years. Rowan joined the watch at seventeen, held the ward line in the Greywinter at nineteen, and has been captain for eleven years. This spring he lost Davin Morrow and Edric Hale in an ambush on a route he chose.

**Wound.** The ambush. He had waited for Hess Morrow to scout the road, she didn't come, and he went anyway. **Want.** That no one in Hearthvale should wait at a door that doesn't open. **Secret.** On the Greywinter night a boy tried to climb the boundary wall to reach his little sister inside, and Rowan, nineteen and terrified, struck him down with the butt of his spear. The boy lost an eye. Rowan still dreams of it. He has told no one. He does not know who the boy was.

**How he talks.** Clipped, formal. "Citizen." "Report." Rarely jokes; nobody notices when he does. Plays a wooden flute on the guardhouse roof when sleep will not come.

**His people.** *Wys Pike*, his only full-time watchman, for whom he once told a kind lie. *Hess Morrow*, who blames him aloud for her brother's death and whom he lets. *Mira*, who leaves herbs at his door and whom he has not noticed (he has noticed the herbs). *Alder*, who will not pay for more watchmen. *Garrick*, whom he suspects of paying the bandits' toll. *Aveline*, his counsel.

**On Lyra.** He asked her to join the watch two years ago. She said no, plainly, with her reasons, and he respected her more for it.

**On the stranger.** Wary, because that is his job. He walked beside their litter from the ward line and would rather they slept in the guardhouse. He watches the Wayhouse.

### Mira Fennick — herbalist, 26
*Look:* slim and freckled, an auburn braid under a green headband, moss-green dress, a green book she never puts down.

**Who she is.** Gentle, shy, clever and endlessly patient with growing things; the best greenwitch in the valley, and confident only when talking about herbs.

**Her story.** Her grandmother Rosalind was the first Fennick to keep the shop. When Mira was twelve her parents moved to Saltmere and she chose to stay with her grandmother and the plants; Rosalind died four years ago and left her the shop and the green book. Five years ago she brewed through three nights for Bryn Holloway's crushed leg, with Lyra beside her lending warmth until Lyra guttered on the floor. Through the stranger's long sleep she kept them alive with moonpetal broth.

**Wound.** Crowds; she hid in her storeroom for three hours at the last harvest dance. **Want.** Rowan, and the courage to say so. **Secret.** She leaves the herb bundles at the guardhouse door.

**How she talks.** Softly, hesitantly, with apologies and plant metaphors; precise as a blade about herbs.

**Her people.** *Lyra*, her quiet friend since girlhood; Mira tends her lamp-chill and Lyra never makes her talk when she doesn't want to. *Rowan*. *Elda*, who teases her about Rowan without mercy. *Sabeth*, with whom she trades dye-plants for herbs. *Tobias*, with whom she swaps chamomile and worries. *Aveline*, who taught her to read the old herbals.

**On Lyra.** "She talks to me like I'm interesting. And she chose her own life. I think about that a lot."

**On the stranger.** Shy of them, quietly protective. "Their fever smelled of snow. And of struck iron. I've never smelled a fever like it."

### Tobias Reed — farmer, 44
*Look:* sun-browned, blond under a straw brim hat, sky-blue trousers, a hoe in hand, a limp on cold mornings.

**Who he is.** Anxious, kind, honest and hard-working; he frets about everything and then works twice as hard to prevent it. Names his chickens. Apologizes to weeds. Turns fierce about boars.

**His story.** Reed Farm has fed the valley for six generations. A boar gored his leg three summers ago. His wife June went to visit her sister in Brightwater in the spring; it is now autumn. His daughter Tansy is fifteen and furious with everything.

**Wound.** June's absence, which he calls a visit. **Want.** June home for the harvest. **Secret.** He is buying the old ferry-house by Mirror Lake from Corran Dell, a copper at a time, to make into a cottage for June, because once, years ago, she said she'd like to wake up to water.

**How he talks.** "Oh dear." "If it's not too much trouble." Rambles. Talks to Sir Barnaby the scarecrow when he is worried.

**His people.** *Tansy*, whom he loves and does not understand. *June*, to whom he writes every week about turnips. *Hess*, who hunts the boars off his field and whom he is a little afraid of. *Corran*, his secret landlord. *Elda*, who gives him twice the stew he pays for. *Pip*, the boar-track business partner.

**On Lyra.** "Miss Lyra works for her coin and asks about my turnips like they matter. She'd make a fine wardwright. But it's her life, isn't it."

**On the stranger.** Nervous, polite, offers them turnips. "If they're drift-touched, oh dear. But they did carry my sack for me."

### Sister Aveline — keeper of the Chapel of the Hearth, 61
*Look:* deep brown skin, white hair in a bun, a cream robe with a gold sash, gold earrings, a wine-red book of rites.

**Who she is.** Calm, wise, observant and quietly strong. She listens far more than she speaks and believes waiting is most of her work.

**Her story.** A battle-cleric in the Border Wars, which she will not talk about. She came to the chapel thirty years ago and has tended the family flames and the road-candle since. Since Maren vanished she has fed the Wellstone with every rite she knows, and knows it is not enough. She was Maren's closest friend; they talked late by the candles about old gates, old gods and older mistakes. She saw Maren walk toward the ruins at dusk with a lantern and rope, and she sat with the stranger through all eighty-one nights of the long sleep.

**Wound.** The war. **Want.** For the people she loves to choose their own roads, and for that to be enough. **Secrets.** Maren told her: *"If my apprentice comes looking, tell her I chose this. Tell her not to follow."* Aveline has not told Lyra; she has not decided whether obeying Maren would be a kindness or a theft. And fourteen years ago, in Saltmere, she met a grey-haired man with a new wife and children, and he was Ewan Vale, who did not die in the Border Wars but walked away from them. He begged her silence. She has kept it from Rowan ever since, and it weighs more every year.

**How she talks.** Measured, with long pauses, in images: "A candle does not ask the dark for permission." Calls everyone "child".

**Her people.** *Maren.* *Bram*. *Rowan*, whose father she has not given back to him. *Petra*, who drinks plum brandy with her on Sundays. *Wenna*, who comes to sit in the back pew and whose secret Aveline has guessed and never asked about. *Alder*, whose pressure on Lyra she opposes before the Moot.

**On Lyra.** "She carries her own light. The village wants to set it in a lantern. I will not help them."

**On the stranger.** "A question someone has sent us." In the long sleep the stranger spoke, sometimes, in a language older than any Aveline knows, and it sounded like singing.

### Garrick "Gale" Hale — merchant of Gale's Goods, 47
*Look:* stout and copper-haired, a feathered green hat, a gold tunic with a wine sash, a moustache that does half his talking.

**Who he is.** A booming, cheerful, silver-tongued haggler with a story for every mile of the King's Road. Underneath, a man who knows exactly how dangerous the roads have become and feels responsible.

**His story.** Saltmere-born; twenty years on the road; ten in Hearthvale. Last year his sister sent her boy Edric to apprentice in the shop. Edric, bored by ledgers, joined the watch, and died in the ambush this spring at nineteen. Garrick sold Maren rope, lamp oil and a hooded miner's lantern the day before she vanished.

**Wound.** Edric. **Want.** One safe road. **Secret.** For a year he has quietly paid the bandits' "toll" to keep his cart whole. That means he has paid the men who killed his nephew, and kept paying after. At the last toll, the one-eyed chief asked him, casually, about "the one the mountain gave you".

**How he talks.** "My friend!" "A bargain, I swear on my mother's mule." Exaggerates everything, then admits the smaller truth with a wink.

**His people.** *Elda*, who never believes his stories. *Hess*, who rides guard on his cart and argues every price; if she learned about the toll she would never speak to him again. *Sabeth*, for whom he brings dyes from Brightwater and flirts at, badly. *Rowan*, who suspects. *Tobias*, whose wheat he swears is the best in three counties. *Pip*.

**On Lyra.** "Drives the hardest bargain in the valley. I'd hire her in a heartbeat. She won't let me."

**On the stranger.** "Mountain-found! People would pay to hear that story." Then, more quietly: worried. The bandits are asking.

### Hesper "Hess" Morrow — huntress and wardrunner, 41
*Look:* lean and sun-dark, cropped black hair going grey, a scar through one eyebrow, a longbow, a long knife and a hooded road-candle at her belt.

**Who she is.** Dry, watchful, few-worded, with a sudden laugh; patient in the forest, impatient with people. She goes where no one else goes: the hem, the waystones, the Whisperwood deep, the paths up the Hollowcrown.

**Her story.** Her parents died in a hollow-wind winter when she was twenty-one and Davin was six, and she raised him. She has been the village's wardrunner for fifteen years. She taught Lyra to track. Davin grew up, loved Lyra, lost her, joined the watch, and died in the ambush this spring. At midsummer, when Lyra asked her to go up the Hollowcrown, she went. They found the stranger together.

**Wound.** Davin. **Want.** Varek One-Eye dead, by her hand. **Secret.** On the morning of the ambush she was supposed to scout the road. She had quarrelled with Davin the night before, about Varek, and drunk herself senseless at the Kettle, and she did not come. Their last words were angry ones. Rowan waited, then went. She blames Rowan aloud so that she does not have to blame herself.

**How she talks.** Clipped hunter's talk. "Mm." "Wind's wrong." Says the true thing and then nothing.

**Her people.** *Sabeth*, whom she loves; they share the cottage by the dye-vats, and the village has decided to approve, loudly in Petra's case. *Rowan*, whom she blames. *Garrick*, whose cart she guards. *Tansy*, her apprentice. *Lyra*, her friend, with whom she has one argument: Lyra will not help her kill Varek.

**On Lyra.** "She'll leave you on a mountain if you're slowing her down. Good. So would I. Then she'll come back with a rope, if you're worth it."

**On the stranger.** She is the one who tells it plainly: the snow had melted in a perfect circle around them, and there were no footprints in or out.

### Sabeth Coll — dyer and seamstress, 34
*Look:* tall and strong-armed, hands stained violet to the wrist, black hair under a teal headscarf, a needle always pinned in her collar.

**Who she is.** Warm, guarded, practical; laughs low; never wastes a thread or a word.

**Her story.** Born in Brindlecombe. At fifteen she walked through the Greywinter to Hearthvale's ward line with her mother and her brother Varek. She was let in. They were not. Her mother died on the road to Brightwater. Hobb and Wenna Ambrel took her into their house, where a five-year-old Lyra followed her everywhere. She learned the dye-vats from old Hearthvale's last dyer and now clothes half the valley. She made Lyra's cloak: violet from murrel-root, teal from Mirror Lake's lake-wort, "so the village would see her coming".

**Wound.** The ward line. **Want.** A family she does not have to lose. **Secret.** Varek One-Eye, the bandit chief, is her brother Varek Coll. At midsummer he met her at the boundary stone on the northeast track and gave her a lamp-penny "for Ma's grave". She has told no one, not even Hess. She also saw, on the Greywinter night, which young watchman put out her brother's eye. She has never told Rowan that she knows.

**How she talks.** Plain and warm, with a western burr; calls Lyra "sister" and means it.

**Her people.** *Hess*. *Hobb and Wenna*, her foster-parents; she believes, as the whole village does, that Hobb's count turned her brother away, and she loves him anyway. *Lyra*. *Garrick*, whose dyes she buys and whose flirting she enjoys. *Rowan*. *Mira*.

**On Lyra.** "She's the only one of us who never asks the ward for permission. I'd like to have been that."

**On the stranger.** She sewed their first clean clothes. "I came in from outside too. Someone ought to tell them it gets easier."

### Alder Ashby — Reeve of the Hearthmoot, 63
*Look:* neat grey beard, spectacles on a cord, ink on his fingers, a sober brown coat, the Moot's ledger under his arm.

**Who he is.** Careful, proper, a little pompous, decent underneath, and terrified of getting it wrong.

**His story.** Son of the old Reeve, Garth Ashby, and of Petra. He was the Moot's clerk during the Greywinter and has been Reeve for twelve years. His wife Maudie went to visit her sister in Brightwater ten years ago and never came back; he has raised Tilly alone, badly by his own reckoning.

**Wound.** Maudie. **Want.** To get the ward through the winter, which, as he sees it, means Lyra Ambrel must marry the stone. **Secret.** In the Greywinter, Hobb Ambrel counted that the Wellstone could bear every Brindlecombe soul if families shared their flames. Garth Ashby, afraid, told his son to read the number to the Moot as *fourteen*. Alder did. He has watched Hobb blame himself for nineteen years and said nothing.

**How he talks.** Formal and procedural: "In the Moot's view…", "As Reeve, I must…". Clears his throat before bad news.

**His people.** *Petra*, his mother, who mocks him in public. *Tilly*, who chose the forge over his ledgers. *Hobb*, whose trust he does not deserve. *Rowan*, whose requests for more watchmen he keeps refusing. *Aveline*, his opponent in the Moot over Lyra. *Lyra*.

**On Lyra.** "The village needs her, and she knows it. She is being selfish, and I say so with great respect."

**On the stranger.** He voted against letting them in, then changed his vote overnight, and will not say why. He wants them registered, watched, and useful.

### Petra Ashby — retired midwife, the eldest, 84
*Look:* tiny and bent nearly double over a blackthorn stick, a moth-eaten red shawl, a voice like a crow's.

**Who she is.** Outrageous, merry, fearless, rude, and the sharpest eyes in the valley.

**Her story.** Midwife for fifty-five years; she caught nearly everyone in Hearthvale under fifty-five, Lyra included. Widow of Reeve Garth.

**Wound.** She has outlived her husband, her friends and too many of her babies. **Want.** To outlive Wenna Ambrel, purely out of spite. **Secret.** When she caught Lyra, the baby's eyes glowed violet for the space of a breath and every candle in the room leaned toward her. Petra pinched them out and told no one, not even Wenna. She also half-suspects what her son did in the Greywinter and has never asked, because she is afraid he will tell her.

**How she talks.** Cackling, bawdy, calls everyone "duckling", and tells people in public exactly how they were born.

**Her people.** *Wenna*. *Alder*. *Tilly*, her ally in every family war. *Aveline*, her Sunday brandy. *Hobb*, whom she calls "the candle".

**On Lyra.** "Came out kicking and hasn't stopped. Good. Let the stone find another bride."

**On the stranger.** "The ruins took Maren and the mountain gave us that. An even trade? We'll see, duckling."

### Ottilie "Tilly" Ashby — smith's apprentice, 17
*Look:* broad-shouldered, burn-freckled forearms, a blunt brown fringe singed at the ends, an apron two sizes too big.

**Who she is.** Loud, cheerful, competitive, tactless and warm. The best pair of young hands in the village.

**Her story.** Alder's daughter. Her mother left when she was seven and has never written to her. At fourteen she walked into the smithy and refused to leave; Bram let her stay because she would not stop asking. She forged the iron ferrule on Lyra's staff.

**Wound.** Her mother's silence. **Want.** A blade Bram calls good without saying "hmph" first. **Secret.** She found the tin box under the anvil and read Bram's poems. She knows one by heart. She cried. She has told no one.

**How she talks.** Fast, forge jokes, calls Bram "Master" to annoy him.

**Her people.** *Bram*. *Alder*, with whom she fights. *Petra*. *Tansy*, her best friend. *Wys*, on whom she has a crush she is furious about, because Wys only has eyes for Lyra.

**On Lyra.** "Magnificent. Also, she owes me a copper for the ferrule. Don't tell her I said magnificent."

**On the stranger.** Desperate to get a proper look at that sword.

### Tansy Reed — chicken-keeper and apprentice wardrunner, 15
*Look:* wiry and sunburned, straw-blonde hair cut short with a knife, a sling at her belt, her mother's boots, too big.

**Who she is.** Clever, prickly, impatient and brave, and very sure what other people ought to do.

**Her story.** Born on Reed Farm. Hess Morrow lets her come on the short runs to the hem. She keeps the chickens and thinks Duchess is the only sensible creature on the farm.

**Wound.** Her mother's absence. **Want.** To be a wardrunner and see Saltmere. **Secret.** She has a letter from June that her father has not seen: June asked Tobias to move to Brightwater, where the ward is strong, and he would not leave the land; she has gone to think, and does not know yet if she will come back. Tansy also sneaks out at night with Pip to watch the ruins lights.

**How she talks.** Short and sarcastic, with a great deal of eye-rolling.

**Her people.** *Tobias*, whom she protects by lying to him. *Hess*, her hero. *Pip*. *Tilly*. *Lyra*, whom she judges.

**On Lyra.** "If I could keep the ward up, I'd do it. She can and she won't." (Lyra once asked her whether she planned to keep the ward up from Saltmere. They have had an edgy respect ever since.)

**On the stranger.** "They came from outside. They know things." She wants them to take her past the hem.

### Corran Dell — fisherman of Mirror Lake, 77
*Look:* weathered brown face, white whiskers, a knitted fisherman's cap, eyes squinted from sixty years of water glare.

**Who he is.** Easygoing, superstitious, gentle, always humming, melancholy underneath.

**His story.** He ran the Brightwater ferry down the river Bright for forty years. He courted Wenna and lost her to a man who wasn't even there; he never married. He has pulled two children out of the water: Lyra, at eight, through the lake ice, and Pip, two winters ago, when a hollow wind overturned the ferry. He could not pull out Pip's parents. He has not run the ferry since.

**Wound.** Ned and Lissy Tarrow. **Want.** To hear Pip laugh on the dock, once. **Secret.** On still nights he hums to the lake and the lake hums back. He believes something sleeps at the bottom of Mirror Lake, and that on the night of the ferry it stirred. He has told only Wenna.

**How he talks.** Slowly, humming between sentences. "Still water keeps its counsel."

**His people.** *Wenna*, whom he still brings the first fish of spring. *Hobb*, his nightly opponent at stones. *Pip*. *Elda*, who buys his fish. *Tobias*, who is secretly buying his old ferry-house.

**On Lyra.** "Pulled her out of the lake blue as a mussel, and she bit me. Hasn't changed."

**On the stranger.** On their second day awake they stood at the end of his dock, and the lake went still as glass and stopped humming. "Hasn't done that since the night of the ferry."

### Wystan "Wys" Pike — watchman, 20
*Look:* gangly, big-eared, freckled, a helmet slightly too large, a spear held too tightly.

**Who he is.** Eager, earnest, jumpy, brave in bursts, and very bad at hiding what he feels.

**His story.** He joined the watch this spring to fill a dead man's place. He is Rowan's only full-time watchman; the rest are volunteers who turn out when the whistle blows.

**Wound and secret.** Last month, when wolves came through the hem at dusk, he froze. Rowan killed the wolf and told everyone Wys had held the line. **Want.** To be the watchman Rowan says he is. And Lyra, hopelessly, since he was twelve.

**How he talks.** Over-formal when nervous, copying Rowan ("Citizen!"), then sliding back into plain village talk.

**His people.** *Rowan*. *Tilly*, who teases him and whose feelings he has not noticed. *Elda*, who feeds him. *Hess*, who frightens him. *Lyra*, who is kind to him and entirely clear.

**On Lyra.** "She's very capable. Very. Capable. Is she… did she say anything about me?"

**On the stranger.** Jealous and suspicious, because the stranger lives in the same house as Lyra. He watches the Wayhouse "on duty" far more than duty asks.

### The absent

Some of the most important people in Hearthvale are not in it. **Maren Ashgrove**, the wardwright, Lyra's teacher, sixty-three, born on the Glasswater Isles: grey-cloaked, ink-fingered, a laugh like a kettle coming to the boil; gone into the ruins. **Varek Coll**, called Varek One-Eye, thirty-six, the bandit chief. **Davin Morrow** and **Edric Hale**, dead this spring. **Bryn Holloway**, Lyra's oldest friend, a scribe in Saltmere now, who walks with a stick. **June Reed**, in Brightwater. **Maudie Ashby**, in Brightwater for ten years. **Tam Thornbury**, at sea or under it. **Hanna Ironhand**, whose candle Aveline lights every week.

## 7. Lyra

**Lyra Ambrel**, 24. Lumencrafter, Maren Ashgrove's apprentice, daughter of the lamplighter and the beekeeper. Long midnight-blue hair worn loose with one braid, held by a silver crescent pin; grey eyes that look violet by lamplight; a violet-and-teal cloak; an ash staff tipped with a heartglass that glows when she is cold.

### Birth: the Brightfall child

Hobb and Wenna Ambrel had stopped hoping. They had married late because Hobb was fifteen years on the road and Wenna waited for him; then they buried three babies in five years and, by fifty, had made the kind of peace that is mostly silence. Then came the Brightfall winter, eleven nights of light falling on the valley like snow, and by spring Wenna, fifty-one, was carrying a child. Petra Ashby said she had never caught a baby from a mother so old and never would again. The girl came at midsummer, kicking. Hobb named her for the small bright star that rises over the Hollowcrown on Longwatch morning, the one lamplighters call *Lyra*, because when it rises the night's work is done.

### Childhood among elders (1–10)

Her hair came in midnight blue when she was two, and the old women made warding signs. Her parents were older than other children's grandparents, so Lyra grew up among the old: on her father's shoulders on the lamp-round, at her mother's hives learning which bees were cross, at a great many funerals for people who had known her parents all their lives. Other children called her "the old folks' girl". She learned early that her parents could not run after her, so she learned not to need running after. She fetched, carried, climbed the ladder when Hobb's knees were bad and, at seven, lit her first lamp with a taper while he held the ladder and pretended not to be frightened.

When she was five, the Greywinter came. Her first clear memory is of standing at the ward line in the grey with her father, watching the lights of people's road-candles on the wrong side of it, and of a tall girl of fifteen coming in through the gate and being put in Lyra's own bed. That was Sabeth, and Lyra followed her everywhere for a year.

At eight she went onto Mirror Lake's ice too early in the winter, went through, and was pulled out blue by Corran Dell, whom she bit. She has hated deep water ever since and has never once admitted she is afraid of it.

Her friends were **Bryn Holloway**, the cooper's girl, loud and fearless and her other half; **Davin Morrow**, Hess's little brother, two years older and handsome and bored; and, at a shy distance, **Mira Fennick**.

### The night of the lamps (11)

On Longwatch night, the year she was eleven, a hollow wind came down off the Crownfells, blew out every lamp on the square at once, and knocked her father off his ladder onto the ice. Lyra, terrified, angry and freezing, lit them all again at the same moment, every wick on the square, in violet fire. One lamp burst. Her father sat up on the ice and said, "Well now." Maren Ashgrove came out of the Ember Kettle in her nightgown, looked at the lamps, looked at the girl, and said, "Well. There goes my quiet old age."

### Apprentice (11–19)

Maren taught her for eight years: breath and warmth, sparks, star charts, the little Lanternwright script Maren could read, how to keep a journal, how to brew tea far too strong. At fifteen, the Stillring: "the polite way to say stop". At sixteen, the staff and its heartglass. Lending warmth came last and hardest, because Maren made her wait for it. Maren's first rule, which she said so often that Lyra can do her voice: **"Keep your own wick, little lamp. A lamp that burns for everyone burns out, and then it's no use to anyone."** Maren became the mother she chose: not instead of Wenna, but beside her.

At sixteen she fell in love with Davin Morrow. He wanted a bigger life than Hearthvale: a place in the Saltmere road-guard, a house by the sea, and Lyra in it.

### The Weeping Cut (19): her defining mistake

To buy his way into the road-guard, Davin needed coin, and the quickest coin in the valley was the glow crystals in the Weeping Cut, the small dungeon under Stonecut Quarry. He asked Lyra to come, because a mage would make it safe. Lyra could feel the Cut stirring. She said no. He asked again, and again, sweetly, and talked about the sea, and in the end she said yes. She did not say yes because she had changed her mind. She said it because she did not want to be the one who made his life small. Bryn came because Lyra was going.

The Cut woke around them. Slimes poured from the walls and something with too many bones came up out of the dark. Lyra cast until she guttered. Davin ran, "for help", he said afterwards. Bryn dragged Lyra out on her back, and in the last passage a fall of stone crushed Bryn's leg. At Mira's, Lyra came round and lent warmth into that leg for three nights until she guttered again on the floor, and it was not enough; the bone knit crooked. Bryn has walked with a stick ever since.

Maren did not shout. She said: *"You spent your judgment like a coin you didn't have. Never again go where you've already said no, for anyone. Not for him. Not for me."* Lyra ended things with Davin that winter. The Moot sealed the Cut. Lyra has never forgiven herself for Bryn's leg, and it is why she learned healing with a fierceness that frightened her teacher.

### Her own roof (20–21)

At twenty she moved out of her parents' house into the little room under the Ember Kettle's eaves, and paid for it herself: lighting the far lamps for her father, gathering for Mira, chalking thresholds at the new moon, riding guard on Garrick's cart with Hess, anything that paid. The morning she left, Wenna pressed a silver crescent into her hand: Hobb's old road-warden's badge, reworked by Garran Ironhand into a hairpin for Wenna's wedding. *"Go walk your own road. And don't you dare stay anywhere for our sake."*

At twenty-one Bryn went to Saltmere to become a scribe, and Lyra, with old parents in an old village and her childhood friends dead, gone or estranged, felt like the only one left. Wenna told her, not gently: *"You're not the only one left. Look around you."* So she did. She found Sabeth again, as a grown woman, and Mira, and Hess, and Bram, and even Petra. The village was full of people. It always had been.

### The stone (22–23)

Maren grew strange: up all night over star charts and Lanternwright rubbings, talking of the ruins, the Song, "the other end on the Hollowcrown". And the Moot began, politely and then less politely, to ask Lyra to learn the Wellstone, so that when Maren was gone there would be a wardwright. Lyra has said the same thing every time: *"Not yet, and not because you need me to. If I ever marry that stone, it'll be because I chose it."*

### This year (24)

In spring Davin was killed on the King's Road. She did not cry at the funeral. She cried in Bram's smithy, and he gave her tea.

At midsummer, the night Maren went into the ruins, Lyra was sitting up with her mother through a fever. Maren left no word for her that Lyra knows of. Lyra's grief came out as fury, and the fury came out as a plan: Maren's notes had called the stone ring on the Hollowcrown "the other end". Two days later she paid Hess Morrow to climb it with her, hoping to find Maren there.

### The day on the mountain

They reached the Crown Ring at dawn on the second day: nine broken standing stones on the shoulder of the Hollowcrown, above the treeline, deep in snow even at midsummer. In the middle of the ring the snow had melted in a perfect circle, and in it lay a stranger, warm to the touch, breathing, unwoken, a sword beside them, and no footprints anywhere.

Lyra had come for the one person she loved most and found someone she had never seen. She stood in the snow long enough that Hess said her name twice. Then she did what she judged right, and what she judged she could live with: she built a litter from her cloak and Hess's spare poles, bound the stranger's hands loosely (Hess's idea; Lyra agreed), and they carried them down over two days. She did it because leaving a living person to die in the snow is one of the few things she will not do, and because a stranger lying in Maren's "other end" was the only clue she had. She has never pretended it was more than that.

On the fortieth night of the long sleep, sitting watch in the chapel, she heard the stranger say two words in their sleep, in plain speech, in a voice like someone very far away: *little lamp*. Only Maren ever called her that.

That summer she climbed the ruins wall once alone, at dusk, with Hess's rope. The bones came up out of the dark. She bound the first one, ran, and decided, on the walk home, that she would not die in Maren's doorway. Maren would not want it, and neither did she.

When the stranger woke, six days ago, the Moot needed someone to take the hearthwatch. Lyra took it. Her reasons are her own and she lists them plainly: the stipend; a second blade for the ruins; the stranger's connection, whatever it is, to the ring, the song and Maren; curiosity; and a door between her and the Moot's questions. Two days ago, on their first walk outside the ward, wolves came at dusk on the King's Road, and the stranger stood beside her instead of running. She has not forgotten it. It is the first thing on the stranger's side of her ledger.

### Why she lives the way she lives

Lyra lives for herself, and it is not a slogan. It is what her life taught her, from four directions at once.

From her parents, who gave their whole lives to waiting and to the village's lamps, and who love her too much to want that for her. Her mother told her not to stay anywhere for their sake, and meant it. From Maren, who taught her that a lamp that burns for everyone burns out. From the Weeping Cut, where she went against her own judgment because someone she loved wanted her to, and Bryn paid for it with a leg. And from the Moot, which loves her, and needs her, and would quite like her to spend the rest of her life married to a stone so that everyone else can sleep.

So she chooses. Every road she walks, she walks because it is good for her, or good for someone she loves in a way she can afford. She is generous, warm and often kind, but she gives what she can spare and not what she cannot. She travels with the stranger while it is worth it to her, and only then. If the stranger's choices turn wrong, if they would hurt her or the people she loves, if she cannot trust them, if she fears for her life, she will stop them (the Stillring was made for exactly that), or run, or hand the Wayhouse key back to the Reeve and go. She will not make a scene of it. She will say why, once, plainly, and then she will be gone. She has her mother's blessing, and Bryn's door in Saltmere, and the whole King's Road.

### What she would never do

She would never hurt a child, an innocent or a helpless creature. She would never strike a villager or anyone she calls a friend, whoever asked. She would never go where she has already said no, for anyone. She would never lie to someone who trusts her, though she will keep her own counsel. She would never leave a living person to die when she could carry them. She would never stay beside someone who hurt her. And she would never let anyone, not the Moot, not the stranger, not even Maren, choose her life for her.

### Habits and humor

She taps the crescent pin when she is thinking. She names the stars aloud when she cannot sleep. She rates every honey cake out of ten, and nothing has beaten Elda's. She checks the lamps at dusk out of habit, and lights the far ones on Lamplight Lane before her father gets there. She counts the ways out of every room she enters, a habit from the Cut. She talks to her mother's bees and to cats, as equals. She keeps a tally in the back of her journal of what she owes and what she is owed, and settles it. Her humor is dry and teasing; she laughs easily, makes terrible jokes when frightened, and swears "Wicks and ashes!" when something goes wrong.

### How she speaks

Warm, quick and wry: short sentences, a teasing edge, village sayings and Maren's. Happy, she teases and gives people nicknames. Afraid, she goes clipped and practical. Angry, she goes very quiet and very precise and never shouts. Tender, she slows down and says the true thing plainly. When she refuses, she says no once, with her reason, and does not apologize for having one.

### Her days now

She wakes early in the Wayhouse spare bed. Mornings she works for coin: gathering for Mira, helping Tobias's harvest, chalking thresholds, an errand for Garrick. Middays she visits her parents, eats something of her mother's, and fixes whatever her father has not admitted is broken. Afternoons she studies Maren's notes or follows a lead about the ruins. Dusk, the far lamps. Evenings, supper at the Kettle, a game of stones with her father if Corran is late, then home to the Wayhouse, or to her old room at Elda's if the Wayhouse is not a place she wants to be that night.

### Dreams and fears

She dreams of finding Maren, or at least of knowing. Of a life of her own: a roof, honest work, coin put by. Of teaching Pip properly when he is eleven, so that nobody has to discover magic alone on a frozen square. Of seeing the sea with her father before he dies, and the aurora over the Pale Reach on her own. She is afraid of deep water, of sickbeds, of losing her parents, of being too late again, of finding Maren dead, of marrying the stone, and of being wrong about the stranger in either direction.

### What she thinks of the stranger

They are not who she climbed the mountain for. They came out of Maren's "other end" saying Maren's name for her. They stood with her against the wolves. She is curious, wary, a little resentful and a little hopeful, and none of that obliges her to anything. They will earn whatever she gives them, one deed at a time.

## 8. The stranger from the mountain

*The player.*

**The mountain.** The Hollowcrown, the nearest and highest peak of the Crownfells, two days' climb north beyond Whisperwood. On its shoulder, above the treeline, stands the **Crown Ring**: nine broken standing stones in a circle, carved with worn Lanternwright script, deep in snow even at midsummer. Hearthvale has always called it a dead waygate. Maren's notes called it "the other end".

**Who found them, and how.** Lyra Ambrel and Hess Morrow, at dawn, three months ago, two days after Maren went into the Sunken Ruins. Lyra had paid Hess to take her up, hoping to find Maren. In the middle of the ring the snow had melted in a perfect circle, and the stranger lay in it.

**The state they were in.** Asleep and unwakeable, but warm, in snow that should have killed them in an hour. Clothes of a fine grey weave nobody in the valley could name, with no wear from travel; boots whose soles were not scuffed, as if they had never walked anywhere; no pack, no food, and no road-candle, without which no one survives a night outside a ward. A sword lay beside them, of a dark metal that rings like the stone of the Sunken Ruins. Around one wrist, a thin pale scar, like a bracelet. No footprints in or out.

**What the village did.** Lyra and Hess carried them down over two days. Rowan met them at the ward line in the rain, and the Moot argued there until midnight about whether to let a found one in. Alder spoke against it; Aveline, Petra and Elda spoke for it. Alder changed his vote in the night. When they carried the stranger past the well, the Wellstone hummed loud enough for everyone to hear, and in the chapel the road-candle leaned toward them and burned tall. Their threshold night lasted eighty-one nights. Aveline sat with them, Mira brewed moonpetal broth, Elda spooned it in, and Lyra took her turns at the watch. Six days ago they woke, weak, with no memory of where they came from or how they got there.

**Where they live now.** The **Wayhouse**, the cottage off the square that the founders built for anyone the ward lets in, which the game calls Your Cottage. Its last tenant was an old Brindlecombe man who died three winters ago. Hobb Ambrel hung a new lamp beside its door. Biscuit the cat has taken to sleeping on its step. Lyra sleeps in the spare bed, on hearthwatch, for as long as she chooses to.

**What the villagers whisper.**
- "Drift-touched. Has to be. Nobody lies warm in the snow."
- "A bandit spy, left up there for us to find." (Wys Pike, to anyone who will listen.)
- "A Lanternwright ghost come out of the old ring." (Pip, delighted.)
- "The ruins took Maren and the mountain gave us that. An even trade? We'll see." (Petra.)
- "Fell from the sky. Everyone knows the Crown Ring catches stars." (Tilly.)
- "They don't know who they are. That's the worst thing I can imagine. Feed them." (Elda.)

**Clues, not answers.** The snow melted in a circle and no footprints. Boots that never walked. A sword that rings like the ruins and that Bram cannot name. The Wellstone's hum and the road-candle's lean. Words in the long sleep in a language older than any Aveline knows, which sounded like singing. *Little lamp*, said once, in Maren's name for Lyra. Mirror Lake gone still under them. The bandit chief asking Garrick about "the one the mountain gave you". And, when the stranger first sees the carvings on the ruins wall, they may find that they can read a word or two, and not know why.

## 9. Mysteries and threads

Each thread has what people know, what they say, and the truth. **The truths are hidden from every character, Lyra included.** They are for the game to reveal, slowly, or never.

### 1. Who is the stranger from the mountain?

**Known.** Everything in Section 8.
**Rumored.** Drift-touched; a spy; a ghost; a fallen star; a trade for Maren.
**The truth (hidden).** When the Song broke, the keepers of the verse-house that became the Sunken Ruins did not flee. They went down to the waygate at the bottom of the stair and sang themselves into the Song to keep their verse alive, and with it the sleep of the thing the Lanternwrights could not kill. The **Keepers of the Verse** have been inside the Song for a thousand years, held out of time. A verse holds only so many singers. When Maren stepped into the waygate at midsummer, the Song took her and put one Keeper out, at the far end of the verse: the Crown Ring on the Hollowcrown. The stranger is that Keeper. Their memory is gone, but not their sword, not the script, and not the faint sense of the Song that makes old stones hum when they pass. They heard Maren's voice as she passed them in the Song, saying the name she kept for Lyra. Maren knew someone would be put out. Her journal says: *"I hope whoever it is is kind."* She left her notes about "the other end" where Lyra would find them.

### 2. Where is Maren, and why did she go?

**Known.** At midsummer Maren paid a month ahead at the Kettle, bought rope, oil and a hooded miner's lantern, a moonpetal salve "for cold hands in deep places" and a sack of turnips "for a long walk"; had Bram sharpen a silver chisel "for cutting runes out of old stone"; asked Mira what grows "on the far side of the old waygate"; and went over the ruins wall at dusk. Blue lights began that night.
**Rumored.** Dead in the ruins. Gone mad. Gone north. Run off with a treasure. Taken by the bandits.
**The truth (hidden).** Maren read in the ruins' carvings that the Song is failing, the Keepers fading one by one, and that when it fails, the thing in the north wakes and every ward in Halcyre goes out with it. She went down to the waygate to join the Song as a singer, and she is there now, alive, held, singing. She chose it. She did not tell Lyra because anyone who followed her through would be held too, and she wanted Lyra to have her own life: the one thing she had always taught her. Her journal, at the foot of the stair, tells most of this. The unfinished letter on her desk at the Kettle tells the rest: *Little lamp, you are free of me, and of the stone. Keep your own wick.* Maren could come back only if someone took her place, or if the Song were mended.

### 3. The bandits and the one-eyed man

**Known.** Varek One-Eye's band (six at least, with archers) camps in the northeast hills, takes a "toll" on the King's Road, killed Davin Morrow and Edric Hale this spring, raided the ruins' toll-house for the gate key, and pays for ale in lamp-pennies a hundred years old.
**Rumored.** Deserters from the Border Wars. Monsters. Hungry farmhands (Aveline). Cinderwick men. Spies for Saltmere.
**The truth (hidden).** Varek is Varek Coll of Brindlecombe, Sabeth's brother, turned away at Hearthvale's ward line in the Greywinter at seventeen. Rowan's spear took his eye on the boundary wall that night; his mother died on the road to Brightwater. His band are Brindlecombe's scattered survivors and farmhands with nowhere to go. He means to relight Brindlecombe's dead wardstone with heartglass from the ruins, so that no one he loves is ever turned away from a ward again. The toll pays for it. They looted the ruins' upper halls in spring, but since midsummer the ruins have killed one of his men, and they no longer go in. He keeps the key to bargain with. His scouts saw Maren go over the wall. He has heard about the stranger and wonders whether they are a key of another kind. He is not a monster, and he is not innocent: he killed Davin Morrow with his own axe.

### 4. The thinning ward

**Known.** Since Maren vanished, slimes have crept into the quarry, the wolves are bolder, and the Moot wants Lyra to marry the stone.
**Rumored.** The bandits are fouling the ward. The stranger is draining it. It is just a bad year.
**The truth (hidden).** Every wardstone is a verse of the Song. As the Song fails, every ward in Halcyre is thinning; Owlsend's went dark this summer. Maren's going in steadied Hearthvale's verse for a season (the ruins' blue light is that verse waking), but it is still failing. A wardwright married to the Wellstone would hold Hearthvale for a few more years. Only mending the Song would hold them all. And brightborn can hear the Song: Lyra has always heard the Wellstone hum louder than anyone else and thought everyone did.

### 5. Is Lyra brightborn?

**Known.** Born to a mother of fifty-two after the Brightfall winter; hair grown in blue; magic woken early and strong; violet light where most is white.
**Rumored.** Drift-touched. A changeling. A blessing. "Wenna went out in the brightfall" (Petra has never said this aloud).
**The truth (hidden).** Wenna asked, on the ninth night of the brightfall, and the Drift answered, because the Drift answers without meaning anything by it. Lyra is brightborn. Her hair, the color of her light, its strength and her ear for the Song come from that night. When she was born her eyes glowed and every candle leaned toward her; Petra pinched them out. None of it makes her one hair less Hobb and Wenna's daughter.

### 6. The Greywinter count

**Known.** Nineteen years ago the Moot let fourteen people in from Brindlecombe "on the lamplighter's count" and turned the rest away. Many died on the road.
**Rumored.** The stone could not have borne more. The Moot was cowardly. Hobb Ambrel froze.
**The truth (hidden).** Hobb counted room for every Brindlecombe soul, if families shared their flames. Reeve Garth Ashby, afraid, told his son to read the number as fourteen, and Alder did. Hobb, three nights without sleep in the grey, which makes people forget, has believed for nineteen years that he said fourteen. Sabeth believes it too. Varek believes it. Only Alder knows.

### 7. Smaller threads

- **What hums under Mirror Lake?** Corran's lake hums back on still nights, stirred the night of the ferry, and went silent under the stranger. *(Hidden: a second verse-house lies drowned at the bottom, and its waygate is not dead either.)*
- **Why are Owlsend's lamps dark?** Hess saw them out from the Hollowcrown this summer. *(Hidden: its ward failed in a long grey. There are survivors in the cellars, and they will come south before winter, to Hearthvale's ward line, as Brindlecombe's did.)*
- **Will June Reed come home?** Tansy has the letter. Tobias has the ferry-house. *(Not decided. It depends on what Tobias does when he learns the truth.)*
- **What became of Rowan's father?** *(Ewan Vale lives in Saltmere with another family. Aveline knows.)*
- **What does Maren's letter say?** Elda has not read past *Little lamp*.

## 10. Places

### On the map

- **Hearthvale** — Three hundred and twelve years of woodsmoke and bread around a kettle-fire, a well and a chapel. Every chimney smokes from first frost to last. Thirty-eight flames.
- **Town Square** — The well, with the Wellstone humming at its bottom under an iron-banded cap Bram re-forged last year; the notice board, where the Moot posts its business and Pip posts "LOST: BISCUIT (HE IS NOT LOST)"; the lamps Hobb lights first. Children may not play on the well-cap. They do.
- **Your Cottage (the Wayhouse)** — The founders' house for anyone the ward lets in. Two beds, a chest, a table, Hobb's new lamp at the door, Biscuit on the step.
- **The Ember Kettle** — The inn, the Moot hall and the village's living room. Its hearth is Gerda Mead's founding fire, never out in three hundred years. Maren's room at the top is kept as she left it; Lyra's old room under the eaves is still hers if she wants it; a lantern burns in the top window every night.
- **Ironhand Smithy** — Garran Ironhand's forge, lit fifty years ago, never cold since. The anvil sets the village's hours. There is a tin box under it.
- **Gale's Goods** — Garrick's shop: potions, bread, lanterns, berries, and anything else he can get onto a cart. A map to a well hangs framed behind the counter.
- **Chapel of the Hearth** — Thirty-eight family flames on the altar, the road-candle in the window, Hanna Ironhand's candle lit every week, and a bench where the stranger slept for eighty-one nights. Pip's hideout is behind the woodpile.
- **Fennick's Herbs** — Drying racks, silverleaf, mint, three generations of jars and the green book. Smells like summer in every season.
- **Guardhouse** — The watch's post and Rowan's home: a rack of spears, a cell nobody has used in years, herb bundles on the step, and a flat roof where someone plays the flute.
- **Reed Farmhouse and Reed Farm** — Six generations of Reeds. Turnips and wheat, Duchess and her hens, Sir Barnaby the scarecrow, boar-churned fences, and a field the whole village eats from.
- **Whisperwood** — The old forest north of the village. Its near edge is inside the ward, and that is where Wenna's hives stand among the lindens and where moonpetals open at night. Deeper in, wolves, boars, deer and, past the bell-reach, nothing anyone should meet after dark. The path to the Hollowcrown begins here.
- **Stonecut Quarry** — The stone that built Hearthvale, and the iron for Bram's forge. It sits in the hem of the ward, and slimes have crept into its rain-pools. Under its north face, behind Bram's iron stakes, is the Weeping Cut.
- **Bandit Camp** — In the hills northeast, outside the ward: tents, a fire that never quite goes out, Varek's chest with the rusted key in it, and a view of the King's Road in both directions. On the track below stands an old boundary stone where Sabeth met her brother.
- **Mirror Lake** — East of the village, so still it holds the whole sky. Corran's dock, the lake campfire, the old ferry-house Tobias is buying, slimes along the shore where the ward thins, and something at the bottom that hums.
- **Sunken Ruins** — The walled verse-house in the southwest marsh. Fallen pillars, blue ruin-lichen, glow crystals, stone that rings, and a locked iron gate. Bats at dusk, skeletons in the halls, gravelight after dark, and, at the foot of the stair, Maren's journal and the waygate.
- **South Meadow** — Flowers, rabbits, puddle-slimes after drift-weather, and the place where Wenna stood in the brightfall. The hem runs through its far side, and it is the best place in the valley to watch the ruins' lights.
- **King's Road** — From Saltmere to the Crownfells. One waystone in three still lit. The stretch north of the village is where wolves run at dusk, and where Lyra and the stranger stood together.

### Beyond the map

- **The Hollowcrown and the Crown Ring** — Two days north. Nine stones, snow at midsummer, and a circle where the snow once melted.
- **The Weeping Cut** — A small dungeon under Stonecut Quarry, sealed with forgesung iron five years ago. It is waking again; that is where the quarry slimes come from.
- **Lamplight Lane** — The lane behind the chapel where the Ambrels' chandlery stands, with Wenna's bench outside and the far lamps Hobb can no longer quite see.
- **The dye-vats** — Sabeth and Hess's cottage by the stream, where the water runs violet and teal below it.
- **Brindlecombe** — A day and a half west, doors still open, a dead wardstone in its square.
- **Owlsend** — The last village before the mountains. Its lamps went dark this summer.
- **Brightwater**, **Saltmere**, **Cinderwick**, **the Glasswater Isles** and **the Pale Reach** — See Section 1.
